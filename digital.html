Digital Literacy

Helping people build confidence, independence and practical skills with technology.

Teaching Digital Skills

I teach digital literacy to adult learners, including people who are new to computers, older learners and people from culturally and linguistically diverse backgrounds.

My approach is practical and supportive. Rather than expecting learners to simply remember instructions, I like to give them opportunities to practise, experiment and solve small problems themselves.

I aim to make technology feel less intimidating by breaking tasks into manageable steps and allowing learners to build confidence gradually.

Lessons & Activities

Mouse & Cursor Skills

I use simple interactive activities to help learners become comfortable controlling the mouse and understanding what happens when they move over different parts of the screen.

One activity involves asking learners to hover over objects without clicking and describe what changes. This gives learners an opportunity to practise mouse control while learning the difference between hovering, clicking and selecting.

PowerPoint — Learning by Doing

I use PowerPoint as a way of combining digital literacy with creative activities. Learners practise selecting objects, moving them around the screen, changing text and working with images.

Activities are designed so that learners can focus on one new skill at a time while gradually becoming more comfortable navigating the program.

Microsoft Word

Word activities focus on practical tasks that learners can use outside the classroom, including typing, formatting text, creating documents, saving files and finding documents again.

I also introduce the Ribbon and other interface features gradually so that learners can understand what the different tools are for rather than simply memorising where to click.

Microsoft Excel — Holiday Planner

I developed a practical Excel project where learners create a holiday planner while learning spreadsheet skills.

The activity introduces formulas, charts and dropdown menus while giving learners a project with a clear purpose and visible outcome.

The project can be extended by introducing additional spreadsheet features as learners become more confident.

Web Browsers & Online Research

I introduce learners to the basic functions of a web browser and help them become more confident navigating websites and finding information online.

Activities include understanding tabs, using search engines, recognising links and navigating between different websites.

Google Maps — Practical Digital Skills

I use familiar real-world tasks to make digital skills relevant and useful. Google Maps activities can be used to practise searching, clicking, zooming, navigating and interpreting information displayed on a screen.

These activities also provide an opportunity for learners to practise skills repeatedly without the activity feeling like a traditional computer lesson.

Digital Safety & AI Literacy

I teach a dedicated digital safety unit, helping learners develop the knowledge and confidence to recognise risks and make safer decisions online.

I regularly look for new resources, research and learning materials that can keep digital safety education relevant as technology changes. This has included exploring resources from organisations such as Google and Khan Academy and adapting useful material into my teaching.

One of the most popular topics in my classes is identifying AI-generated and manipulated content. Learners explore how AI can appear in images, written text, video and voice, and practise looking for clues that can help them question whether something is genuine.

I see digital safety as an area that needs to continually evolve. New technologies create new opportunities, but also new forms of misinformation, scams and manipulation. I therefore try to keep learning myself and bring current, practical examples into the classroom.

Spotting AI — Images, Text, Video & Voice

Learners examine examples of images, text, video and voice and look for clues that something may have been generated or manipulated.

The activity encourages curiosity and healthy scepticism rather than simply assuming that something online is either real or fake.

Focus: AI Literacy / Critical Thinking Media: Images / Text / Video / Voice

My Approach

Digital literacy is not just about knowing which button to press. For many learners, the bigger challenge is developing the confidence to explore technology without being afraid of making a mistake.

I therefore try to create activities where learners can experiment safely, ask questions and practise skills repeatedly. I use clear demonstrations, visual instructions and practical projects to help learners connect individual computer skills with things they actually want to do.
